It’s no coincidence that companies like Alphabet, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, and NVIDIA show up on both **Forbes' America's Best Companies** ([Schwarz & Newman, 2025](https://www.forbes.com/lists/best-companies/)) and **Fortune's Most Innovative Companies** ([Fortune Media IP Limited, 2026](https://fortune.com/ranking/americas-most-innovative-companies/)). Creativity and innovation are directly tied to sales growth, competitive advantage, and long-term survival. Organizations that continuously improve their products, services, and ways of working are far less likely to become obsolete ([Acar et al., 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0149206318805832); [Reiter-Palmon & Dredge, 2021](https://books.google.es/books?hl=en\&lr=\&id=4h1QEAAAQBAJ\&oi=fnd\&pg=PA460\&dq=long-term+survival+and+innovation+Reiter+Palmon\&ots=vKXlCh408O\&sig=DbUoR5mzGgmpIMSdW8tyZKKvolM#v=onepage\&q\&f=false)).

That is not particularly surprising. What is worth asking, however, is what it actually means to be creative, what it means to be innovative, and where creativity fits into that picture. Those words appear constantly in strategy documents, leadership presentations, job descriptions, and mission statements. They have become so common that they often function more like buzzwords than clearly defined concepts. Yet understanding the difference matters, especially for managers responsible for improving how their teams work, make better creative judgments, and strengthen their own creative thinking.

## Creativity and Innovation Are Not the Same Thing

Psychological research has spent decades answering this question (e.g., [Hunter et al., 2013](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780203499733-5/leadership-innovation-technology-samuel-hunter-lily-cushenbery-nicole-ginther-joshua-fairchild); [Mumford & England, 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2713374522000176)). It does not just define creativity and innovation. It explains what drives each one and what it takes to move an initial idea to a new product, service, or process. That is what we want to unpack here: what these constructs are, how they differ, and why understanding both can help managers think more creatively themselves while also building more innovative teams.

***Creativity*** is the generation of ideas that are novel and useful ([Amabile, 1988](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Performance/Amabile_A_Model_of_CreativityOrg.Beh_v10_pp123-167.pdf)). One of the biggest misconceptions is that creativity means wild originality. It does not. **Creativity is novelty with a point.**

***Innovation*** is what happens afterward. It is the successful implementation of creative ideas into products, services, processes, or other meaningful organizational outcomes. Whether that happens depends not only on the quality and originality of the idea itself, but also on the conditions surrounding it ([Mumford, Bedell-Avers & Hunter, 2008](https://www.emerald.com/books/edited-volume/13959/chapter-abstract/84832088/Planning-for-innovation-A-multi-level-perspective?redirectedFrom=fulltext)).

## Where Innovation Begins

Every innovation starts with an idea. Understanding how creative ideas are generated gives leaders practical ways to improve innovation before implementation even begins. Instead of hoping employees come up with better ideas, you can help them frame problems, gather better information, encourage diverse thinking, facilitate thoughtful idea evaluation, and create an environment where stronger ideas are more likely to emerge ([Hunter & Cushenbery, 2012](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1523422311424263)). Just as importantly, managers can use the same insights to improve their own thinking. They also solve complex problems, interpret ambiguous information, evaluate options, and decide which ideas deserve attention. In that sense, creativity is not only something they should support in others. It is something they need to practice themselves.

Cognitive process models show that creativity is not magic or a spontaneous flash of inspiration. People draw on information, expertise, and experience to build ideas that are both new and useful. Like any process, creativity can be understood, developed, and better supported ([Puccio et al., 2018](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jocb.381);[ Scott, Leritz & Mumford, 2004](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400410409534549)). While there is much to discuss (and we will, in future posts), research suggests that creative thinking generally involves the following stages ([Mumford et al., 1991](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400419109534380)):

#### Define the problem.

Creative thinking begins with identifying and defining problems worth solving ([Reiter-Palmon & Robinson, 2009](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2009-01867-010)). Help employees clarify the problems that matter most rather than jumping prematurely to solutions ([Rietzschel, Nijstad & Stroebe, 2014](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400419.2014.901084)). **Better problem definition often leads to better ideas.**

#### Gather information.

Creative ideas are built from knowledge and experience. **The broader and more diverse the information available, the stronger the raw material for rich ideas** ([Kohn et al., 2011](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Nicholas-Kohn/publication/254301561_Conceptual_Combinations_and_Subsequent_Creativity/links/0046353985a2997c0f000000/Conceptual-Combinations-and-Subsequent-Creativity.pdf)). Support this by exposing teams to different perspectives, customers, and expertise ([Boira-Lopez & Connelly, 2026](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2027-25744-001)) and deliberately widening what you read, who you talk to, and which viewpoints you consider.

#### Generative alternative ideas.

People create novel solutions by combining existing knowledge in new ways ([Scott, Lonergan & Mumford, 2005](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326934crj1701_7)). **Encouraging multiple ideas before selecting one generally leads to stronger outcomes** ([Puccio & Cabra, 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/abs/pii/B9780123747143000094)). Create space for exploration before pushing teams toward convergence and resist the temptation to settle too quickly on the first answer that feels comfortable.

#### Evaluate ideas.

**Creative ideas are refined through thoughtful evaluation using criteria such as originality, usefulness, feasibility, and strategic value** ([Watts, 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2023.2229153)). Facilitate constructive feedback based on criteria and avoid unintentionally narrowing creativity by evaluating ideas too early or too harshly.

#### Select and pilot the strongest idea.

Once an idea has been refined, the next step is to test it on a small scale before broader implementation ([Baer, 2012](https://journals.aom.org/doi/abs/10.5465/amj.2009.0470)). **Encourage low-cost experimentation and learning from early results.** This is not just about protecting the team. It is also about helping themselves think in a more iterative and less all or nothing way.

#### Monitor and refine.

Creative work is iterative ([Sawyer, 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2713374521000029)). Feedback and new information often lead to further improvements rather than a finished solution ([Tolkamp et al., 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2023.2234719)). **Reinforce this process by treating iteration as progress rather than failure and by giving teams the time and psychological safety to improve ideas over time.**

### The Idea Is Only the Beginning

Here is the catch. A strong creative idea is the foundation, but it is only the foundation. Even excellent ideas can quietly disappear in a supervisor's inbox or an overlooked project folder. Trust me, I’ve experienced that more than once. This is where a leader’s role becomes critical.

Team dynamics influence whether people feel safe enough to share their ideas in the first place (e.g., [Reiter-Palmon, Wigert & de Vreede, 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/edited-volume/abs/pii/B9780123747143000136)). If employees expect criticism, dismissal, or embarrassment, many ideas will never be voiced ([Sacramento et al., 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jpim.12699)). Leadership matters just as much (e.g., [van Kinppenberg, 201](https://www.elgaronline.com/edcollchap/edcoll/9781784715458/9781784715458.00027.xml)7). Will managers dismiss an idea because it feels risky or unfamiliar? Or will they help refine it, champion to senior leadership, remove obstacles, and secure the resources needed to test it? Organizational culture and climate also play an essential role (e.g., [Hunter, Bedell & Mumford, 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10400410709336883)). Does the organization actually reward innovation, or just talk about valuing it? Does it prioritize hiring and developing creative, cognitively diverse people? Does it pay attention to changes in its environment so it can stay ahead rather than constantly reacting?

## One Final Thought

Creativity and innovation are not the same thing. Creativity is a necessary precondition for innovation, but it is only the beginning. Innovation depends on what happens after an idea is generated. Whether that idea becomes a better product, service, or process depends largely on the environment built around it.

Leaders play a central role in creating that environment. By encouraging idea generation, facilitating thoughtful evaluation, championing promising ideas and helping teams secure the resources needed to experiment and improve, they transform creativity from isolated moments into sustained innovation. But that is only part of the story. The same knowledge also helps managers become better creative thinkers themselves. It improves how they frame problems, generate options, evaluate possibilities, and make decisions under uncertainty.

**The most effective leaders do not simply support creativity in others. They understand how creativity works well enough to apply it in their own thinking and to build systems, teams, and cultures that turn ideas into innovation.**
